The light switch

A small box that sits over my bedroom light switch and presses it with a servo, so I never had to touch the mains wiring. The final version has a 24 GHz radar that notices when someone is sitting still at the desk, an OLED screen, two touch pads, five modes and app control.

The blinds controller

A stepper motor that pulls the bead chain on my existing roller blind, so the blind didn't need replacing. I designed a PCB in KiCad to tidy up the wiring. It runs its own schedules, including one that opens the blind slowly in the morning and one that closes it once the light switch reports the room has been empty for a while.

  • ESP32-C6
  • TMC2209
  • KiCad
  • Fusion 360
  • C++

Zaylo Remote

The app that controls the switch and the blinds. It's a web app you can install on a phone. Devices update live over MQTT, accounts and shared homes run on Firebase, and if a device drops off Wi-Fi I can reconnect it over Bluetooth without taking it off the wall. The write-up has a demo you can try with pretend devices.

  • JavaScript
  • PWA
  • MQTT
  • Firebase
  • Web Bluetooth

2025–nowA-Level Computer Science project

Atlas, a self-balancing desk robot

A two-wheeled robot that has to keep itself upright. I've switched it from PID to an LQR controller, with the gains worked out in MATLAB from a model of the robot. When it isn't balancing, its job is to be a revision timer on my desk.

Controlling an inverted pendulum

I wrote this to properly understand the maths behind Atlas. It starts with PID, then builds up LQR from the state-space model: the A and B matrices, controllability, the Riccati equation and a worked example with real numbers.

About me

Born in Tbilisi, grew up in Bristol.

I started the light switch when I was 13. It was meant to be a quick fix for my room, but it turned into my excuse to learn CAD, electronics and programming all at once.

I'm now doing Maths, Further Maths, Physics and Computer Science. Atlas, the balancing robot, is my Computer Science project, and it's the reason I ended up reading about state-space models and the Riccati equation.

Most of my projects go through Fusion 360 for the enclosure, KiCad if there's a board, and C++ on an ESP32. I use Python or MATLAB when I want to check the maths before I trust it on real hardware.

Away from the desk I play water polo four times a week and tennis once a week, and I game when there's time left over. I want to study electrical engineering, and since the week at Rolls-Royce I've been looking at degree apprenticeships as well as university.

Experience

Work experience, university programmes and school.

  1. October 20265–9 October

    Rolls-Royce, Bristol Latest

    Work experience week

    Talks, site visits and a group project, covering engineering, advanced manufacturing, project management and aviation safety.

    • Went round the CTF, where Rolls-Royce makes the composite fan blades for UltraFan, and sat in on a seminar about how aviation safety investigations work.
    • Built a project in a team of five and presented it to Rolls-Royce engineers for feedback.
    • Finished at Aerospace Bristol, following the engines from early piston designs up to the Olympus 593 from Concorde.

    Hearing from Alexia Williams MBE, who started at Rolls-Royce as a degree apprentice, and from the apprentices there now, made me take that route seriously.

  2. 2025One week

    MBDA, Filton

    Engineering work experience

    A week with the engineering teams at MBDA. The biggest thing I took away was how the mechanical, electrical and software engineers on one project depend on each other, and how much of the job is documentation: every design decision is written down and checked against the standards they work to before anything moves forward.

    I also got to look at some of the electronics that go into their systems, which is the side of engineering I enjoy most. At the end of the week I put together a concept for a future missile and presented it to senior staff.

  3. 2023One week

    University of Bristol

    Work experience in the maths department

    A week in the maths department working on problems beyond the school syllabus. I wrote Python scripts to work through the maths, then learned LaTeX to write the results up as a proper research-style document, with the files organised the way the researchers there keep theirs.

    It was my first real look at how maths is actually done at university.
