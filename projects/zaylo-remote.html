Zaylo Remote

The app that runs my light switch and blinds. It's a web app you can install on a phone, with live updates over MQTT, shared homes on Firebase, and a Bluetooth rescue screen for when a device loses its Wi-Fi.

One place for everything

Once I had a light switch and a blinds controller, I wanted one place to control both. The dashboard opens straight away with the last state it saw, then updates as MQTT messages arrive from the devices.

Firebase handles sign-in and shared homes, so everyone in the house can use the same devices from their own phone and see the same state.

The other problem it solves is Wi-Fi. A new router or a changed password can leave a device offline, and without the app the only fix would be opening the case. Instead the setup screen connects to the device over Bluetooth, sends it the new details and shows its diagnostics.

How the app connects

There are three separate routes: Firebase for accounts and shared homes, MQTT for controlling the devices live, and Web Bluetooth for setting them up or rescuing them.

What's going on inside

One state store

A single store holds the devices, the home and the user. When a device changes, or someone else's phone sends a command, every screen updates from it. sessionStorage carries the state between pages.

Accounts and shared homes

A home groups the devices that belong together, and invites let other people in the house join with their own account. Firestore rules only let members of a home see its devices.

Live updates over MQTT

Commands go out through the broker and the app listens for state coming back, so the controls stay in step with the hardware. How fast that feels depends on the connection.

Bluetooth rescue

In a browser with Web Bluetooth, I can connect straight to a nearby device, read its logs and give it new Wi-Fi details. It's my way back in when it can't reach the router.

Demo mode

The demo uses pretend switches and blinds, so anyone can try the controls without touching my hardware. The blind positions move, but they aren't coming from a real motor.

Installable

A service worker caches the app's files and it can be added to the home screen. Live updates still need the network, and the browser has to keep the page running.

The screens

Most of these are in the demo at the top of the page.

  1. Home

    Every device with its live status, a switch between shared homes, and the invite flow for adding the rest of the household.

  2. Light switch

    On and off, the five modes, the alarm, the radar settings and schedules for the presence automations.

  3. Blinds

    A slider for any position from closed to open, saved favourite positions, sunrise and sunset schedules, single-step nudges, and recalibrating the limits if the chain changes.

  4. Spaces

    Scenes, groups and automations that control several blinds at once.

  5. Setup and rescue

    A step-by-step pairing flow over Bluetooth: pick a network, enter the broker details and check the device answers. Nothing to install.

  6. Diagnostics

    Live MQTT traffic, round-trip times, the service worker's state and the Firestore sync log. It's the screen I open when something looks wrong.

The hardware on the other end

I wrote the app for these two. Their pages go into the hardware and firmware properly.

The light switch →A servo presses my bedroom light switch, with radar presence sensing, touch pads and five modes. The blinds controller →A stepper motor pulls the bead chain of my roller blind, on a PCB I designed.
