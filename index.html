Giorgi Suramlishvili: My project portfolio

I’m a sixth-form student in Bristol and this is where I write up what I make. It started when I was 13 with a box that presses my light switch using a servo. Since then I’ve built a motor for my blinds, an app to run both of them, and a robot that balances on two wheels.

About me

Born in Tbilisi, grew up in Bristol.

I started the light switch when I was 13. It was supposed to be a quick fix for my room. It ended up being how I learned CAD, electronics and programming, mostly all at the same time.

Now I’m doing Maths, Further Maths, Physics and Computer Science. Atlas is my Computer Science project, and it’s the reason I ended up reading about state-space models and the Riccati equation in my spare time.

Most things start in Fusion 360 for the case, then KiCad if there’s a board, then C++ on an ESP32. If there’s maths involved I check it in Python or MATLAB first, because being wrong on a laptop is a lot cheaper than being wrong on a robot.

Outside of that I play water polo four times a week and tennis once a week, and game when there’s time left. Next, I want to study electrical engineering.

The light switch

A box that sits over the light switch in my bedroom and presses it with a servo, so I never had to go near the mains. By the third version it had a 24 GHz radar that knows I’m at my desk even when I’m sitting completely still, a small OLED screen, two touch pads and five modes.

The blinds controller

A stepper motor that pulls the bead chain on the roller blind I already had, so the blind itself didn’t need replacing. I designed a PCB for it in KiCad to tidy up the wiring. It opens the blind slowly in the morning, and closes it once the light switch says the room’s been empty for a while.

  • TMC2209
  • KiCad

Zaylo Remote

The app for the switch and the blinds. It’s a web app you can install on your phone. The devices update live over MQTT, logins and shared homes go through Firebase, and if one of them drops off the Wi-Fi I can fix it over Bluetooth without taking it off the wall. Its page has a demo with fake devices you can play with.

  • JavaScript
  • PWA
  • MQTT
  • Firebase
  • Web Bluetooth

2025–26A-Level CS projectBalancing

Atlas, a self-balancing desk robot

A two-wheeled robot that keeps itself upright. It started out on PID and now uses LQR, with the gains worked out in MATLAB from a model of the robot. It’s built and balancing, it doubles as a revision timer for my desk, and I’m writing up the documentation now.

Experience

Work experience, university programmes and school.

  1. October 20265–9 October

    Rolls-Royce, Bristol Latest

    Work experience week

    A week of talks, site visits and a group project, covering engineering, advanced manufacturing, project management and aviation safety.

    • Went round the CTF, where Rolls-Royce makes the composite fan blades for UltraFan, and sat in on a seminar about how aviation safety investigations are run.
    • In a team of five, came up with an idea for making STEM accessible to more people, then pitched it to Rolls-Royce engineers and got their feedback.
    • Finished at Aerospace Bristol, following the engines from early piston designs up to the Olympus 593 from Concorde.

  2. 2025One week

    MBDA, Filton

    Engineering work experience

    A week with the engineering teams at MBDA. What surprised me most was how much of the job is documentation. Every design decision gets written down and checked against their standards before anything moves on, and the mechanical, electrical and software people all rely on each other’s paperwork.

    I also got to see some of the electronics that go into their systems, which is the side I enjoy most. On the last day I presented a concept for a future missile to senior staff.

  3. 2023One week

    University of Bristol

    Work experience in the maths department

    A week in the maths department on problems well past the school syllabus. I used Python to work through them, then learned LaTeX to write the results up properly, laid out the way the researchers there do theirs.

    It was the first time I’d done maths without a textbook telling me which method to use.
