The blinds controller

Using the chain that was already there

The blind already had a chain that worked fine, so I didn't want to replace it. Instead the controller grips that chain: a wheel on the motor pulls the beads round, and the case screws to the wall beside the window.

I can move it from the app, by voice, or with the touch pad on the front. The ESP32-C6 handles the motor, the touch input and the schedules itself. The app goes through MQTT, voice goes through SinricPro, and the weather-based rules need fresh data from the internet. The settings are saved on the device.

How the parts connect

The touch pad goes into the ESP32-C6. The ESP32 tells the motor driver when to step and which way to turn, and the driver powers the motor coils. Wi-Fi connects it to the app and the other services.

Driving the stepper motor

Every step pulse from the ESP32-C6 turns the motor a tiny amount through the TMC2209 driver. I chose the TMC2209 because it's quiet. With StealthChop2 and microstepping it's much less noisy than a basic driver, which matters for something that moves in a bedroom first thing in the morning.

Timing on its own

The steps come from a 20 kHz hardware timer interrupt, separate from the Wi-Fi code, so a busy network doesn't make the motor stutter.

Speeding up gently

Normal moves ramp the speed up at the start and down at the end, so the chain doesn't jerk.

Knowing where it is

There's no encoder. It counts steps from the calibrated top and bottom, so a missed step would make the position it reports slightly off.

Reading the touch pad

One pad does everything on the device itself. What a press does depends on how long you hold it and whether the blind is already moving.

Single tap

Open, close or stop

When it's stopped, a tap opens the blind if it's under 50% open and closes it if it's 50% or more. While it's moving, a tap slows it down to a stop.

Second tap within 400 ms

Go the other way

While it's moving, a double tap stops the current move and starts one in the opposite direction. When it's stopped, it acts like a single tap.

Hold 1.5 s while moving

Stop straight away

Stops the motor without the slow-down and ignores the pad until you let go. Keeping your finger on it won't go on to reset the Wi-Fi.

Hold 10 s while stopped

Set up Wi-Fi again

Clears the saved Wi-Fi details and restarts setup. The travel calibration is kept. Let go once it restarts.

Moves only work once the travel limits are calibrated. A press has to stay stable for 50 ms before the firmware believes it, which filters out short electrical glitches.

The Wi-Fi reset is a separate path. When the motor is stopped, staying in Pressed for 10 seconds clears the Wi-Fi settings and restarts the controller. If a hold has already stopped the motor, it stays in Hold handled until you let go, so the same press can't do both.

Schedules and automations

All five rules run on the controller, but they don't all get their information from it. The fixed schedules use its clock, the sunset and heat rules need weather data, and the empty-room rule depends on the light switch.

  1. Morning wake-up

    Opens the blind in small steps over a period I choose, up to two hours, so the room gets lighter gradually instead of all at once.

    Trigger
    A start time for each day of the week
    Safety
    Only ever moves towards open, and a tap cancels it

  2. Close at sunset

    Gets the sunset time from OpenWeatherMap and closes the blind then, so the schedule follows the seasons without me touching it.

    Source
    OpenWeatherMap, refreshed daily
    Offset
    A few minutes before or after sunset, if I want

  3. Night lock

    Closes the blind at night and stops the other rules moving it until the morning routine.

    Schedule
    Per day, with weekdays and weekends
    Note
    It's a software lock, not a physical one

  4. Close when the room is empty

    The light switch publishes whether the room is occupied over MQTT. If it stays empty for the delay I've set, the blind closes.

    Needs
    The light switch and the broker to be online
    Delay
    Stops it closing every time I leave for a minute

  5. Heat protection

    Closes the blind when the outside temperature from OpenWeatherMap goes above a threshold.

    Source
    Outside temperature, not a sensor in the room
    Cooldown
    15 minutes, so it doesn't keep flipping near the threshold

The network, and saving power

Wi-Fi carries the app commands and the weather and time updates, and Bluetooth is for setup and recovery. The touch pad always works, but anything that needs the network waits until it's back.

Setup and recovery

The Wi-Fi details go in over Bluetooth during setup. Holding the pad for 10 seconds with the motor stopped clears them and restarts setup, without losing the calibration.

Alexa and Google Home

Voice commands go through SinricPro, and the controller reports its estimated position back. This part needs the internet.

MQTT

It publishes its position, motor data and diagnostics to my broker, and subscribes to the light switch so the empty-room rule works.

Weather and time

Sunset and temperature come from OpenWeatherMap once a day. Time comes from NTP, and if a network blocks NTP it reads the time from an HTTP response header instead.

Target Wake Time

Wi-Fi 6 lets the controller and a compatible router agree on wake-up windows, so the radio can sleep in between. The drawing below shows the idea. It isn't a current measurement, and I haven't measured the saving on this build yet.

Protecting the settings

The calibration and schedules live in flash, and a power cut in the middle of a save shouldn't lose them.

  • 2s

    Between saves

    Settings save when they change, but never more than once every two seconds, to save wear on the flash.

  • 2

    Copies, each with a CRC32

    There are two settings slots. At start-up the firmware checks the checksum and loads the other copy if the main one is broken.

  • 3

    Restarts before recovery mode

    Repeated unexpected restarts put it into recovery mode, which stops normal motor moves so I can work out what's wrong before it tries again.

If I change the format of the saved settings in a firmware update, it checks the version that's stored and moves the compatible values across.

DiagnosticsSix ring buffers covering Wi-Fi, MQTT and the motor

What I'd change next

The first thing I want to try is setting up the TMC2209 over UART instead of relying only on its hardware pins. That would let the firmware change the driver settings and read its diagnostics, which should make setup and fault-finding easier.

I'm also thinking about separate up and down buttons and a clearer status light. Those are ideas for the next build, not things the current one does.

In the meantime you can try the blinds controls in the Zaylo Remote demo, which has pretend blinds you can move around.
